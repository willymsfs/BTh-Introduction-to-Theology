Welcome: You Are Already a Theologian

Before You open a single Textbook, You have already done Theology. Every Time You have prayed, doubted, argued about Suffering, or asked what Caste has to do with the Gospel, You were already doing what this Course will now name and discipline. You are not about to acquire a foreign Skill; You are about to become critically aware of a Practice You never actually stood outside of.

Moses did not go looking for God on Horeb; he was tending Sheep, and a Bush stopped him. "I will turn aside and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt" (Ex 3:3). Theology begins in exactly that Turning Aside — an ordinary Day interrupted by a Question that will not let You walk past it.

Three Virtues are asked of You in this Course: Wonder before a Mystery no Definition exhausts, Honesty about the real Plurality of legitimate Theologies rather than a false Search for one tidy Answer, and Commitment — Theology is not a neutral Academic Subject; it is done from inside the believing Community, for the sake of that Community's Mission.

The Leitmotif of the Course: The Burning Bush (Exodus 3)

Every Course needs one Text to which it returns until the Text becomes a Habit of Mind. This Course returns to Exodus 3:1–10, the one biblical Scene that contains, in miniature, everything the six Units will unfold at length.

Eight Readings of Exodus 3

  • 1. The Bush That Burns and Is Not Consumed (3:2). The Chapter itself names this as the Paradigm of God-Experience — Theology begins in an Encounter, not a Syllabus.
  • 2. "Come No Closer; Remove Your Sandals" (3:5). Holy Ground beneath every theological Sentence — the Hinge into Unit 2's Sources and Resources.
  • 3. "I Am the God of Your Father, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob" (3:6). Continuity with a Tradition received, not invented — the Seed of Unit 3's History of Doctrine.
  • 4. "I Have Seen the Affliction of My People... I Have Heard Their Cry" (3:7). The Cry of the Poor as a genuine theological Resource — anticipates the Contextual and Liberation Theologies of Units 3 and 6.
  • 5. "What Is His Name?" (3:13). Moses himself asks for a Definition — the very Question Unit 1 answers eightfold, and no single Answer closes.
  • 6. "I AM WHO I AM" (3:14). The Name that both reveals and withholds — the Root of the Analogical Way and, later, of the Criteria that guard authentic God-Talk in Unit 5.
  • 7. "Take Off Your Sandals" — a Different Reader in Every Age. The Bush has been read by Ephraem, Aquinas, Rahner and Abhishiktananda each in a different theological Key — the Seven Models of Unit 4.
  • 8. "Come, I Will Send You" (3:10). No Encounter with the Bush ends in Contemplation alone; it ends in a Sending — the Ground of Unit 6's Theologizing in the Indian Context.

Indian Context: the Bush is not a distant Semitic Curiosity for the Indian Seminarian. A God who reveals Himself as Fire that does not consume, on Ground that is suddenly Holy though it was ordinary a Moment before, speaks directly into a religious Culture already fluent in Theophany, in Darshan, in the Sudden Sacredness of a Place. The Seminarian does not need to learn what a Burning Bush might mean; he needs only to recognise that he already knows.

Aim, Architecture and Assessment

Aim of the Course

To move the Student from a vague popular Sense of "Theology" to a working critical Grasp of what Theology is, where it draws its Content from, why it has always existed in more than one legitimate Form, how several major Theologians have actually practised it, what marks the Boundary between legitimate Pluralism and Heterodoxy, and what it means to theologize inside the concrete Realities of India.

The Four Movements of the Course

Movement A (Units 1–2): Foundations — what Theology is, and the Sources and Resources it draws upon. Movement B (Unit 3): History — the five Periods that produced today's Pluralism. Movement C (Unit 4): Models — seven Theologians, seven ways of holding Faith and Culture together. Movement D (Units 5–6): Criteria and Context — what authenticates a Theology as Catholic, and what it means to do that Theology in India.

Evaluation

Continuous Assessment (Class Tests, Seminar Presentation): 40%. End-Semester Examination: 60%, including one compulsory Integration Question requiring the Student to trace one Theme across at least three Units.

Primary Texts to Be Kept Open

Kuncheria Pathil and Dominic Veliath, An Introduction to Theology, Indian Theological Series · Vatican II, Dei Verbum and Gaudium et Spes · the Catechism of the Catholic Church on Revelation and the Magisterium.

Unit 1: What Is Theology?

Classroom Cue. Open the first Class by asking the Students to complete the Sentence, Theology is … in five Words. Write eight or ten Answers on the Board. Most will define it as "Study of God" alone; return to the Board after the eighth Definition below and ask what has changed.

Theology = theos + logos, God-Talk — but in a World of Secularism, Agnosticism and Atheism, God's Existence cannot be taken for granted. Three Positions divide the Field: the Atheistic View (Camus, Marx, Nietzsche, Freud — the Problem of Evil, God as Projection); the Agnostic View (the Question itself is not, after all, important); and the Theistic View, which the Course endorses — God as the Ultimate Ground of Being, knowable by natural Reason. Aquinas's Five Ways move from Effect to Cause to Prime Mover, but as Newman cautioned, such Proofs offer only "a convergence of diverse probabilities," not coercive Certainty.

Can Finite Language Reach an Infinite God?

Three Ways of using Words about God: Univocal (the same Word, the same Meaning — impossible for God-Talk); Equivocal (unrelated Meanings — collapses Theology into Nonsense); and Analogical (a Word applying by Proportion, developed by Aristotle and refined by Aquinas). Calling God "our Father" is neither univocal nor equivocal; we purify the earthly Notion of its Limits and apply it to God in an eminent Way.

Eight Definitions — a Series, Not a Sentence

The Chapter refuses a single tidy Definition, stacking eight instead: Theologia (Etymology, from Plato to the medieval University); Faith Seeking Understanding (Anselm); Lex Orandi, Lex Credendi (the Eastern Way); Theological Anthropology (Rahner: "whatever we say about God says something about us"); Critical Reflection on Praxis (Gutierrez, Orthodoxy to Orthopraxis); Theology as Hermeneutics; Interpretation of the Christian Story (mythos); and the Working Definition — systematic, critical, rooted in Revelation, ecclesial.

Classroom Cue. Students often assume the Analogical Way is a Compromise, a weaker Cousin of literal Speech. Point out that Aquinas considered it the only Way that makes Theology possible at all: without Analogy, we would be reduced to total Silence (Apophaticism) or Nonsense (Equivocation). It is a Solution, not a Concession.

Eight Creative Polarities

Theologizing is a set of held Tensions, none resolved by collapsing into one Side: Mystery ↔ Intelligibility · Identity ↔ Change · Committed ↔ Critical · Community ↔ Individual Theologian · One ↔ Many · Universal ↔ Contextual · Orthodoxy ↔ Heterodoxy · Orthodoxy ↔ Orthopraxis. "All theories are tested by praxis; practice in turn challenges the theories that become inadequate."

Indian Context: Upadhyaya, Abhishiktananda and Panikkar used Vedanta, understanding the Trinity through Saccidananda — an Indian ancilla theologiae placed at the Service of the Christian Mystery exactly as the Greek Fathers once placed the Logos.

Review Questions

  1. State the three Positions on God's Existence and give the theological Objection to each of the first two.
  2. Name the eight Definitions of Theology given in this Unit and give the Latin Formula attached to Anselm's.
  3. Choose two of the eight Polarities and show, with an Example from your own Formation, how holding both Sides together (rather than choosing one) has actually shaped your Faith.

Unit 2: The Sources of Theology

Classroom Cue. Divide the Class into three Groups, one each for Scripture, Tradition, and Magisterium, and give ten Minutes to defend why their assigned Source could not stand alone. Bring them back together and use Dei Verbum 10 to show that the Exercise was designed to fail: none of the three can, in fact, stand without the others.

Revelation is God's Self-Communication; Faith is the human Reception and Response — two Sides of the same Event. Vatican II resolved a centuries-long Debate (Luther and Calvin's Sola Scriptura against Trent's "Scripture and unwritten Traditions"): there is, in fact, one Source, the Apostolic Tradition, concretely identified through three linked Realities, "so linked and joined together that one cannot stand without the others" (Dei Verbum, 10).

  • Sacred Scripture — norma normans non normata, the Norm which norms all else and is itself normed by no other Criterion.
  • Tradition and Traditions — six Vehicles: the 22 Individual Churches (India holds two, Syro-Malabar and Syro-Malankara); Liturgy and Sacraments (lex orandi, lex credendi); Creeds; the Fathers of the Church; Dogmas; and Customs, Disciplines, Codes and Catechisms.
  • Magisterium — the College of Bishops in Communion with the Pope.

Resources — Where God Still Speaks

Where the Sources are the normative, historically fixed Media, the Resources are the wider Field read through the Signs of the Times: People and their Experience, Cultural Resources, other Religious Traditions, Peoples' Movements and the Voice of the Marginalized, and the Cry of the Poor. "God speaks today in a very special way through the cry of the poor, their struggles, aspirations, visions and hopes."

Review Questions

  1. State the Vatican II Resolution of the historical Debate on the Sources, with a Citation from Dei Verbum 10.
  2. Name the six Vehicles of Tradition given in this Unit.
  3. Distinguish a Source from a Resource, and give one Example of each drawn from your own Diocese or Region in India.

Unit 3: Development and Pluralism in Theology

Classroom Cue. Students often confuse Pluralism with Relativism, and the Confusion is dangerous enough to name directly before proceeding. Ask the Class for one Example each of a legitimate theological Difference (Pluralism) and a Claim that no Truth exists at all (Relativism), and correct any Answer that blurs the two.

Why is there not one Theology, but many? Five historical Periods, walked in Order. Pluralism means one Truth approached from more than one legitimate Angle; Relativism denies a single Truth exists at all — "a negation of realism."

The Five Periods

Biblical — the Gospels were written backwards: the earliest Preaching was the bare Kerygma, from which grew the Passion Narrative, and only later the Material on Jesus's earlier Ministry placed in front of it, yielding a Matthean, Marcan, Lucan and Johannine Theology. Patristic — three geographic Streams: Semitic (Ephraem), Greek (the Cappadocians), Latin (Augustine); Indian Context: the Syro-Malabar and Syro-Malankara Churches inherit their Ancestry directly from the Semitic-Syriac Stream. Scholastic — Anselm, Bernard, Aquinas, Bonaventure, Scotus, Ockham, four Periods organised around university Disputation, Gilson's "cathedrals of the mind." Post-Scholastic — the Neo-Scholastic Renewal, the Existentialist Thrust, and the Kairos of Vatican II, where Congar, Daniélou, de Lubac and Rahner opened the Neo-Scholastic Mould from within. Contextual — Secularisation, Black Theology, Feminist Theologies, Ecofeminism, Dalit Theology, Minjung Theology.

Classroom Cue. Update this Section each Year before teaching. Ask the Students to name one contemporary Contextual Theology not listed here (Migrant Theology, Ecological Theology in the Sundarbans, and so on) and test it against the Caution that follows.

A necessary Caution: not everything calling itself Contextual is automatically authentically Catholic — tested against the Criteria of Unit 5.

Review Questions

  1. Explain how one Bible contains a Plurality of Theologies, citing the Example of Mark's Gospel.
  2. Name the three geographic Streams of the Patristic Period and one representative Father of each.
  3. Dalit Theology is named in this Unit as one Instance of Contextual Theology. State, with a concrete Example, why it could not simply be imported from Latin American Liberation Theology unchanged.

Unit 4: Selected Models of Theology

Classroom Cue. The Weber-style Line, that Institutions inevitably deaden original Charism, usually wins early Sympathy from the Class, since every Seminarian has seen Enthusiasm settle into Routine in some Parish. Do not refute it outright; concede it describes something Real, then ask which of the seven Models below resists that Deadening most successfully, and why.

Seven Theologians, seven ways Faith and Culture are actually balanced in Practice, presented in historical Order. Four ways to strike the Balance: Translation Model (hands on the inherited Tradition); Anthropological Model (the local cultural Context); Synthetic Model (Tradition and Context, roughly equal); Transcendental Model (the theologizing Subject itself).

  • Ephraem (c.306–373) — Theology as Poetry, refusing Definitions as a Kind of Blasphemy against the Limitless.
  • Schmemann (1921–1983) — Liturgical Experience, "theology is best expressed in the form of praise."
  • Aquinas (1225–1274) — Sacred Science, Philosophy as Handmaid.
  • Barth (1886–1968) — Theology as Response, measured entirely against the Word.
  • Rahner (1904–1984) — Theological Anthropology, the Supernatural Existential.
  • Liberation Theologians (1970s) — Critical Reflection on Praxis, the "non-person" as primary Interlocutor.
  • Abhishiktananda (1910–1973) — Advaitic Experience, Theology as realized Experience "within."

Indian Context: two of these seven Models are chosen precisely for India — Ephraem, Root of the Syro-Malabar and Syro-Malankara Traditions, and Schmemann's celebrative liturgical Model, shared with all Oriental Traditions. Two more arise directly from the Indian Context: the Praxis Model and Abhishiktananda's contemplative Approach.

Review Questions

  1. Name the four ways of balancing Faith and Culture and give one Theologian representing each.
  2. State Aquinas's Understanding of Philosophy as "Handmaid" and cite his own Reason for feeling free to change philosophical Frameworks.
  3. Compare Abhishiktananda's contemplative Model with the Praxis Model, and explain which speaks more directly to your own future Ministry in India, and why.

Unit 5: The Criteria of Christian Theological Reflection

Classroom Cue. Ask the Class what would happen if a Diocesan Priest publicly rejected a Prudential Admonition versus a Definitive Dogma. Use the two Answers to show that Assent is genuinely graded in the Church, not an all-or-nothing Demand, before moving to the four Gradations.

If so many Theologies can be legitimate, where does Pluralism end and Heterodoxy begin? Raimundo Panikkar's sharpest Question: "What is the criterion of the criterion?"

The Magisterium and the Gradations of Doctrine

The Extraordinary Magisterium makes a solemn, infallible Definition. The Ordinary Magisterium covers everything else. Four Gradations of Doctrine, four kinds of Assent: Definitive Dogma requires the Assent of Faith; Definitive Doctrine requires Firm Acceptance; Non-Definitive Authoritative Doctrine, where most Church Teaching actually falls, requires religious Submission of Intellect and Will; Prudential Admonitions call for Obedience to the Spirit of the Guidance.

Eight Parameters of the Catholic Theological Enterprise

The Knowability of God · the Partnership of Faith and Reason · Universality beyond Sectarianism · an essential communitarian Context (sensus fidelium and Ecumenism — India's own three Individual Churches live this Criterion as daily ecclesial Fact) · Catholicity in Time · the sacramental Principle · Revelation transmitted through Scripture and Tradition together · a real Role for Authority.

Review Questions

  1. Distinguish the Extraordinary from the Ordinary Magisterium, with one Example of each.
  2. Name the four Gradations of Church Doctrine and the Assent proper to each.
  3. An Interfaith Dialogue Partner asks how the Catholic Church can accept Pluralism in Theology while still claiming to teach with Authority. Answer using the eight Parameters of this Unit.

Unit 6: Theologizing in the Indian Context

Classroom Cue. This Unit lands the whole Course; do not rush it into the last twenty Minutes of a Class. Ask the Students, before any Lecture, which of the two Streams, Cultural or Socio-Economic, feels more urgent in their own home Diocese, and hold their Answers up again at the Unit's Close.

When the Gospel met the Greeks, Romans and Syrians, it became Greek, Roman and Syrian Theology. Did the same happen when the Gospel met India? Three ecclesial Families: the St Thomas Christians (traditioned to 52 AD, giving rise to the Syro-Malabar and Syro-Malankara Churches), the Latin Rite (from Xavier, 1542, the largest today), and the Protestant Unions (CSI 1947, CNI 1970). Christians remain about 2.5% of India's Population.

Two Streams Converging

One Stream works from Culture, Philosophy and Religion: de Nobili's accommodatio; the Vedanta Pioneers (Upadhyaya, Appasamy, Chenchaiah, Panikkar); the Christian Ashram Movement (Monchanin, Abhishiktananda, Bede Griffiths). The other works from Socio-Economic and Political Realities: Devanandan and M.M. Thomas on Christ and the new Humanity; Kappen, Rayan and Soares-Prabhu's Indian Liberation Theology; Dalit Theology and Women's Theology. The two Streams are increasingly seen as related, not opposed.

Dalit Theology

The Cry of Dalit Christians, doubly discriminated: denied State Benefits reserved for Hindu Dalits, and still facing Prejudice inside Parts of the Church. It rejects the upper-caste philosophical Language of the earlier Pioneers, drawing instead on Dalit "little Traditions" — Myths, Stories, Folklore, Rituals — centred on the Crucified and Risen Jesus. "Where do we find the God of the Bible today? Is He not with the Dalits? Yes, He is the God of the Dalits."

Review Questions

  1. Name the three ecclesial Families in India and give the founding Date or Figure of each.
  2. State the two Streams of Indian Theological Method identified in this Unit, with one Pioneer named for each.
  3. A Parishioner tells You that Caste has nothing to do with Theology, only with Society. Using Dalit Theology's own Argument, write a three-sentence Response.

Closing Unit: The Bush That Sends

We opened with the Bush that burns and is not consumed. This Course's own Itinerary, from the God-Question, through Sources and History, through seven Models and the Criteria that guard them, to the Indian Context in which You will actually minister, is itself an Enactment of Moses' Turn: "I will turn aside and see this great sight" (Ex 3:3). Theology begins in this Turning Aside, in Wonder before the Holy Ground on which the Theologian already stands, and it ends, as Moses' Encounter ended, in a Sending: "Come, I will send you" (Ex 3:10). No Definition given in this Course, from Fides Quaerens Intellectum to critical Reflection on Praxis, is complete without this final Turn toward Mission.
